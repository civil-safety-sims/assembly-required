# VERIFIED SAFETY DATA & SOURCES (STRICT ADHERENCE REQUIRED)

## 1. Tear Gas & Chemical Weapons
* **The Myth:** Using milk, vinegar, or baking soda.
* **The Reality:** These are non-sterile and can cause eye infections.
* **The Fix:** Water (in a squirt-top bottle) or saline solution.
* **The Gear:** Shatter-resistant eye protection (Ballistic goggles).
* **Nuance:** Swimming goggles are okay, but *tight* seals can cause pressure damage if hit.
* **Source:** [Physicians for Human Rights](https://phr.org/our-work/resources/preparing-for-protecting-against-and-treating-tear-gas-and-other-chemical-irritant-exposure-a-protesters-guide/)

## 2. Clothing & Material Physics
* **The Danger:** Synthetic fabrics (Polyester/Nylon).
* **The Reality:** If exposed to fire or flashbangs, synthetics melt *into* the skin.
* **The Fix:** 100% Cotton or Wool outer layers.
* **The Nuance:** "Layering" is critical. If gas hits your clothes, you must be able to shed the outer layer quickly.
* **Source:** [Amnesty International Safety Toolkit](https://www.amnestyusa.org/pdfs/SafeyDuringProtest_F.pdf)

## 3. Arrest & Medical Preparedness
* **The Danger:** Losing access to meds during a 24-48 hour jail hold.
* **The Fix:** Bring 3 days of prescription meds in their *original* bottle (police often confiscate loose pills as "unknown substances").
* **The Menstrual Risk:** Tampons can absorb tear gas chemicals internally. Use pads or cups.
* **Source:** [National Lawyers Guild](https://www.nlg.org/know-your-rights/)

## 4. Digital Hygiene (Advanced)
* **The Danger:** Biometrics (FaceID/Fingerprint).
* **The Reality:** Biometrics are not protected by the 5th Amendment. Police can compel you to unlock. Passcodes are protected.
* **The Fix:** Turn off Biometrics before arriving.
* **Source:** [EFF Surveillance Self-Defense](https://ssd.eff.org/module/attending-protest)

## 5. Identification
* **The Danger:** Tattoos and hair color.
* **The Fix:** Cover unique tattoos with bandages or long sleeves.
* **The Backup:** Write "Legal Aid" number on your arm in Permanent Marker (paper can be confiscated; skin cannot).
* **Source:** [ACLU Protester Rights](https://www.aclu.org/know-your-rights/protesters-rights)
